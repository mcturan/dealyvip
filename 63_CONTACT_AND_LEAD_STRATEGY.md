# Contact and Lead Flow Strategy

## 1. Information-First Philosophy
Users should not be pressured to contact DealyVIP before they understand the limitations of their situation. The strategy requires the user to consume orientation guides (e.g., Verify Supplier Existence, Red Flags) before initiating contact.

## 2. Primary User Journey
- **Entry:** User lands on a Guide or Tool page via search.
- **Consumption:** User understands the boundary between legal registry checks and physical commercial reality.
- **Decision:** User determines whether they need physical coordination, interpreter services, or advanced verification.
- **Preparation:** User is presented with a "Preparation Checklist" to gather facts.
- **Contact:** User navigates to the Contact page.

## 3. Contact Moments
**Appropriate Contact Moments:**
- After understanding the limitations of public records.
- When needing to arrange a factory observation visit.
- When requiring local linguistic support (interpreters).

**Inappropriate Contact Moments:**
- Expecting a legal audit.
- Seeking ISO certifications or certified technical QA.
- Asking DealyVIP to act as a financial guarantor or escrow service.

## 4. CTA Principles
- **Contextual:** CTAs must match the context of the guide (e.g., a guide about visiting a factory shouldn't have a CTA about basic registry lookups).
- **Non-aggressive:** No "Contact Us Now!" language. Instead, use "Prepare Your Information" or "Explore Coordination Options."
- **Informational:** Some paths lead to more guides, not to a contact form.

## 5. Operational Boundaries
Contact channels must strictly filter out requests that violate DealyVIP's operational boundaries (e.g., rejecting requests for "certified quality checks" in favor of "observation visits").
