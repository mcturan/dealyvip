Risk assessment in international trade is about pattern recognition. While no single issue automatically proves fraud, multiple warning signs indicate that you should pause the transaction and investigate deeper.

## What This Guide Helps You Check
- Common behavioral patterns of fraudulent entities.
- Inconsistencies in documentation and communication.
- Financial pressure tactics.

## What Public Information Cannot Establish
- **Absolute Fraud:** Even legitimate businesses sometimes have poor communication or confusing corporate structures. Red flags mean "investigate," not necessarily "cancel."

## Critical Warning Signs

### 1. Inconsistent Corporate Identity
The most common sign of a problem is a mismatched identity. 
- The website says "Apex Global Manufacturing," but the corporate registry says "Apex Trading LLC," and the proforma invoice asks for payment to "Apex Logistics."
- Never send money to an entity that differs from the verified legal manufacturer.

### 2. Changing or Suspicious Bank Details
- The supplier suddenly changes the destination bank account right before payment, citing "audit issues" or "tax optimization."
- The bank account is located in a completely different country than the supplier's registered address (e.g., a Turkish supplier asking for payment to an account in Hong Kong).
- The account name belongs to an individual person, not the corporate entity.

### 3. Refusal of Live Video or Physical Visits
A legitimate factory is usually proud to show off its production line.
- The supplier invents constant excuses (e.g., "company policy," "COVID restrictions," "trade secrets") to avoid a live WhatsApp or Zoom call from the factory floor.
- They aggressively resist the idea of you sending an independent local representative to visit their premises.

### 4. Communication and Pressure Tactics
- Unrealistic urgency: "You must pay the deposit today or we lose the raw materials."
- Unrealistic pricing: The quoted price is significantly lower than the global market average for that commodity. If it looks too good to be true, it almost always is.
- Sole reliance on free email services and messaging apps, lacking any professional domain infrastructure.

## What to Do If You See Red Flags
If you encounter these warning signs, stop the transaction. Request official registry documents, demand a live video inspection, and verify their certificates directly with the issuing bodies.
