Language barriers in international trade cause more than just minor misunderstandings; they lead to incorrect specifications, wrong pricing, and disputed contracts. Relying on an automated translation tool for a $50,000 transaction is an unacceptable risk.

## What This Guide Helps You Navigate
- How to prepare an interpreter for your meeting.
- How to structure your communication.
- The limits of an interpreter's role.

## What Public Information Cannot Establish
- **Legal Nuance:** Unless your interpreter is also a licensed lawyer in that jurisdiction, they cannot provide legal advice on the contract they are translating.

## 1. Prepare Terminology Before the Meeting
Do not expect a general business interpreter to instantly know highly specific industry jargon (e.g., specific chemical compounds, obscure machinery parts, or niche textile terms).
- Send the interpreter a brief document containing your key technical terms and product specifications a few days before the meeting.
- Share the basic agenda so they understand the context of the negotiation.

## 2. Structure Your Communication
- **Speak in short, complete sentences.** Pause frequently to allow the interpreter to translate accurately.
- **Speak directly to the supplier,** not to the interpreter. Maintain eye contact with your business partner.
- **Avoid slang, idioms, or cultural jokes** that do not translate well and may cause confusion.

## 3. Confirm Important Details in Writing
Verbal negotiations are fluid. At the end of the meeting, the interpreter should help you summarize the agreed points (price, quantity, delivery time, Incoterms).
*Action:* Follow up the meeting immediately with a written email summarizing these points, translated into both languages, to create a formal paper trail.

## Important Limitations
An interpreter translates what is said. They are not:
- **Your Legal Counsel:** They cannot advise you if a contract clause is dangerous under local law.
- **Your Technical Advisor:** They cannot verify if the supplier's technical claims about a machine are physically accurate.
- **Your Negotiator:** They should not be making independent deals or decisions on your behalf.
