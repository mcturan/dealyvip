A common risk in cross-border trade is dealing with a "manufacturer" that is actually a trading intermediary, or worse, a fraudulent entity. Verifying a factory requires a layered approach beyond basic legal registration.

## What This Guide Helps You Check
- Is the supplier an actual manufacturer or a trading company?
- Does the factory physically exist?
- Is the supplier capable of fulfilling your order?

## What Public Information Cannot Establish
- **Product Quality:** Documents cannot prove the current batch meets your specifications.
- **Factory Ownership:** A registered address does not guarantee they own the factory at that address.

## The Layered Verification Model

### Layer 1: Corporate Identity
Confirm the company exists legally using their Tax ID and MERSİS number. (See: [How to Verify a Turkish Company](/en/guides/verify-turkish-company/)).

### Layer 2: The Capacity Report (Kapasite Raporu)
If a Turkish company claims to be a manufacturer, they will have a **Capacity Report** issued by their local Chamber of Commerce or Industry (e.g., Istanbul Chamber of Industry - ISO). This official document details their machinery, employee count, and maximum annual production capacity. 
*Action:* Always ask for this document. Trading companies will not have one.

### Layer 3: Digital & Video Verification
Do not rely solely on website photos. 
*Action:* Request a live video call via WhatsApp or Zoom from the factory floor. Ask them to show the production line and current inventory. Scammers and remote intermediaries will usually invent excuses to avoid this.

### Layer 4: Physical Verification
For high-value transactions, remote verification is insufficient. An independent party should visit the address to confirm operations.

## Red Flags
- The supplier claims to be a massive factory but cannot produce a Capacity Report.
- They refuse live video calls from the production floor.
- The factory address is listed in a residential area or a multi-story office building (unless it is light manufacturing like textiles).
- The bank account for the deposit belongs to an individual, not the corporate entity.

## Important Limitations
You cannot publicly search a database of "verified good factories." You must actively request documents from the supplier and scrutinize them. Furthermore, general observation visits cannot replace licensed Quality Control (QC). If you need an ISO-certified technical inspection of goods, you must hire an accredited agency like SGS or Bureau Veritas.
