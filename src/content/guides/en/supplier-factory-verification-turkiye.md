Türkiye is a major manufacturing hub for textiles, automotive parts, machinery, construction materials, and agriculture. However, separating actual manufacturers from trading companies or fraudulent actors requires a careful evaluation process.

This guide provides a framework for finding and evaluating Turkish suppliers.

## Sourcing Channels

When looking for suppliers in Türkiye, international buyers generally rely on:

- **B2B Marketplaces:** Platforms like Alibaba or Turkish-specific export portals. *Caution:* Anyone can create a profile; presence on a marketplace is not proof of manufacturing capacity.
- **Trade Shows and Exhibitions:** Istanbul (TÜYAP, CNR) and Bursa host massive international trade fairs. Meeting face-to-face is one of the most reliable discovery methods.
- **Exporters' Assemblies:** Organizations like TIM (Turkish Exporters Assembly) and regional exporter associations (e.g., İTKİB for textiles) can provide lists of verified members.
- **Chambers of Commerce:** The Istanbul Chamber of Commerce (İTO) or Gaziantep Chamber of Commerce (GTO) maintain member directories.

## Evaluating the Supplier

Once you have identified a potential supplier, you must evaluate them across three dimensions: Legal, Operational, and Financial.

### 1. Legal Identity Check
Never rely solely on a website or a WhatsApp conversation. 
- Ask for their **Vergi Levhası** (Tax Plate) and **Faaliyet Belgesi** (Activity Certificate). 
- Verify their legal name through the MERSİS public lookup.
- Check if their registered address in the trade registry matches the factory address they provided.

### 2. Operational & Factory Verification
Many companies claim to be "manufacturers" but are actually trading intermediaries. 
- **Request a video tour:** Ask the sales representative to do a live video call walking through the production floor. 
- **Check Capacity Reports (Kapasite Raporu):** Real manufacturers in Türkiye are issued a Capacity Report by their local Chamber of Industry (e.g., İSO in Istanbul). Ask to see this document; it lists the exact machinery and production capacity registered to their address.
- **Physical Inspection:** If the order is large, physically visiting the factory—or hiring a local agent to do so—is strongly recommended.

### 3. Product and Reference Checking
- Always order and pay for a physical sample before committing to bulk production.
- Request references from their existing export clients, preferably in your region.

## Warning Signs During Evaluation

Walk away or escalate your due diligence if you notice:
- **Bank Account Mismatches:** The supplier asks you to wire money to an individual's personal bank account instead of the exact corporate name registered in MERSİS.
- **Evasion of Video Calls:** The supplier makes excuses for why they cannot show you the factory floor live.
- **"Virtual" Headquarters:** Their legal address points to a shared co-working space, yet they claim to produce heavy industrial equipment.

## A Note on Payment Risks

There is no universally "safe" payment method that works for every transaction. While Letters of Credit (L/C) offer high security, many mid-sized Turkish manufacturers prefer T/T (Bank Transfer) with a deposit structure (e.g., 30% advance, 70% before shipment). 

If you are paying by T/T, operational evaluation and strict due diligence are your only safety nets.
