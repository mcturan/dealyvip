When you cannot physically travel to a foreign country, sending an independent local representative to visit the supplier on your behalf is one of the most effective ways to mitigate fraud.

## What This Guide Helps You Navigate
- Defining the scope of a proxy visit.
- Instructing the local representative.
- Differentiating between an observation visit and a certified inspection.

## What Public Information Cannot Establish
- **Certification:** An observation visit confirms physical reality; it does not produce a legally binding technical certification of the goods.

## 1. Define the Purpose of the Visit
You must give the local representative explicit instructions. An observation visit is usually designed to verify:
- Does the factory actually exist at the registered address?
- Is it currently operational and staffed?
- Do they have the product you are purchasing in their warehouse?

## 2. Prepare the Request List
Provide the local representative with a checklist of evidence to collect:
- Photos of the exterior building and signage.
- Photos of the production floor.
- Photos of the specific goods being discussed.
- A live video call (e.g., via WhatsApp or Zoom) connecting you directly from the factory floor.

## 3. Informing the Supplier
Inform the supplier that your "local representative" will visit them next week to view the facilities and say hello. 
*Red Flag Test:* If the supplier is fraudulent or a hidden intermediary, they will immediately invent excuses to cancel or delay the visit. Legitimate manufacturers welcome local representatives.

## Important Limitations: Observation vs. Inspection
You must clearly understand the difference:
- **Observation Visit:** An independent professional goes to the location, takes photos, verifies the address, and sets up a video call. They act as your eyes and ears.
- **Accredited QC Inspection:** A certified engineer (e.g., from SGS or Bureau Veritas) tests the tensile strength, chemical composition, or electrical safety of your specific batch of goods using calibrated tools.
