Before engaging in business with a Turkish entity, it is critical to confirm its legal existence. This guide explains how to use official state registries to verify a company's status.

## What This Guide Helps You Check
- Is the company legally registered in Türkiye?
- What is its official Tax Identification Number (VKN)?
- Where is its registered headquarters?

## What Public Information Cannot Establish
- **Commercial Reliability:** A registered company can still have debt or fail to deliver goods.
- **Product Ownership:** Registration does not prove the company is a factory rather than a trading intermediary.
- **Quality Assurance:** Registry records do not guarantee the quality of the goods.

## Step-by-Step Verification Process

### 1. Request the Tax Plate (Vergi Levhası)
Ask the supplier for their Tax Plate. Every legitimate Turkish company has one. It contains their official legal name, registered address, and Tax ID (VKN). 

### 2. Verify the Tax ID
Use the **Interactive Tax Office (İVD)** to confirm the tax number is valid and matches the provided company name. 

### 3. Check the Central Registration System (MERSİS)
The Turkish Ministry of Trade manages **MERSİS**. Every registered company has a 16-digit MERSİS number. While full registry access requires a Turkish e-Devlet (e-Government) login, you should always ask your supplier for their MERSİS number to confirm formal commercial registration.

### 4. Review the Trade Registry Gazette
The **Turkish Trade Registry Gazette (Ticaret Sicil Gazetesi)** publishes official structural changes, such as board appointments and capital increases. You can search the company's historical announcements here.

## Red Flags
- The supplier refuses to provide a MERSİS number or Tax Plate.
- The bank account name for payment does not exactly match the registered company name on the Tax Plate.
- The company was registered very recently, but claims to have decades of manufacturing experience.

## Important Limitations
Foreign buyers face a digital barrier: full remote access to Turkish corporate registries often requires a Turkish citizenship ID. You are heavily reliant on the supplier providing the correct documents.
