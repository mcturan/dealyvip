International trade runs on documents: proforma invoices, ISO certificates, CE declarations, and registration papers. Unfortunately, PDFs are trivial to forge. 

## What This Guide Helps You Check
- How to evaluate document consistency.
- How to verify certificates with issuing authorities.
- How to distinguish between self-declarations and accredited certificates.

## What Public Information Cannot Establish
- **Complete Authenticity:** Unless a document can be verified directly with the issuer, you cannot be 100% certain it has not been altered.
- **Current Validity of Goods:** A genuine CE certificate for a past product does not guarantee the current batch is compliant.

## The Difference Between Existing and Verified
It is crucial to understand that:
- **A document exists:** The supplier sent you a PDF.
- **A document is internally consistent:** The dates, names, and formats look correct.
- **A document is verified:** You have independently confirmed its authenticity directly with the organization that issued it.

## Practical Steps to Verify Documents

### 1. Check Certificates with the Issuer
If a supplier provides an ISO quality certificate or a product testing report (e.g., SGS, TÜV, Bureau Veritas), **do not trust the PDF alone**. 
- Look up the issuing body's official website.
- Enter the certificate number into their official public verification portal.
- If no portal exists, email the issuing agency directly with a copy of the certificate to confirm its validity.

### 2. Analyze Document Consistency
Examine the documents closely for internal contradictions:
- Does the company name on the bank account exactly match the name on the corporate registration?
- Are the dates logical? (e.g., Is the ISO certificate older than the company's registration date?)
- Are there obvious signs of digital manipulation, such as mismatched fonts or blurred text blocks?

### 3. Understand "Self-Declarations"
Many buyers are misled by "Declarations of Conformity." In many jurisdictions, a manufacturer can print their own CE mark or conformity declaration without independent testing. Understand the difference between a manufacturer's self-declaration and a certificate from an accredited independent testing laboratory.

## Red Flags
- The supplier refuses to provide high-resolution copies of their certificates.
- The issuing body of a critical quality certificate is an unknown entity with no online presence.
- The bank details on the invoice belong to an individual or a third-party company in a different country.

## Important Limitations
There is no universal global database of verified business documents. Verification requires manual, tedious work: contacting individual chambers of commerce, testing labs, or registries.
