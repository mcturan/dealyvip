Traveling internationally to visit a supplier is expensive and time-consuming. Arriving unprepared wastes that investment. A factory visit should be treated as a structured audit, not a casual tour.

## What This Guide Helps You Navigate
- Pre-travel verification steps.
- Structuring the agenda of the visit.
- What specific evidence to collect while on-site.

## What Public Information Cannot Establish
- **Immigration/Travel Reality:** We do not provide visa or legal travel advice. You must verify entry requirements independently.

## 1. Verify Before You Fly
Do not travel to meet a company whose basic legal existence you haven't checked. 
- Confirm their registration in the state registry.
- Verify the exact physical address of the meeting. Is it a factory in an industrial zone, or an office in a city center? 
- Confirm exactly who you will be meeting and their legal role in the company.

## 2. Prepare Your Checklist
Define exactly what you need to see. Do not let the supplier control the entire agenda.
- **Production Line:** Ask to see the specific machinery that will manufacture your product.
- **Raw Materials:** Ask to see their current inventory of raw materials.
- **Finished Goods:** Inspect their warehouse for finished goods ready for shipment to other clients.
- **Quality Control:** Ask them to demonstrate their internal QC testing process.

## 3. Documents and Samples
- Bring your detailed product specifications.
- Request physical samples during the visit.
- Ask to review their original Capacity Report (if in Türkiye) or ISO certificates in person.

## 4. Communication Preparation
If you and the supplier do not share a highly fluent common language, hire an independent interpreter. Do not rely entirely on the supplier's English-speaking sales representative, as they control the translation of your negotiations.

## Important Limitations
A visual walk-through by a buyer is excellent for establishing general commercial reality, but it is not a substitute for an accredited technical Quality Control (QC) inspection. If your product requires strict technical tolerances, you must still hire a certified inspection agency before shipment.
